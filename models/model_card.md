## Excluded columns and why

- `status`: a perfect leak. `DELAYED` ⇔ arrival > 24h late on every row; it is the label in another form and is only known after arrival.
- `actual_departure`, `actual_arrival`, `actual_delay_hours`, `transit_days_actual`, `on_time_flag`: post-booking outcomes; the label is computed from them.
- `dq_flags`: written by curation after the fact, and several flags (e.g. `completed_without_actuals`) depend on outcome fields.
- `booking_date`: used only to split; its absolute value never recurs in the future.
- `customer_id`, `vessel_id`, `route_key`: high-cardinality ids whose historical late rate does not carry over in time. Evidence (late rate per group in train vs the same group in test):

| key | groups in train | median train rows / group | groups in both | train→validation corr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `customer_id` | 251 | 11 | 220 | -0.04 |
| `vessel_id` | 80 | 34 | 79 | 0.02 |
| `route_key` | 593 | 5 | 362 | -0.16 |
| `origin_port` | 25 | 112 | 25 | 0.24 |
| `destination_port` | 25 | 112 | 25 | -0.44 |
| `cargo_type` | 10 | 276 | 10 | -0.01 |

With a median of 11 train shipments per customer, a group's late rate is mostly binomial noise (the standard error of a 40% rate on 11 rows is ±15pp), so the correlation sits near zero; even ports (~110 rows each) and cargo types show no carry-over, and their spread of late rates matches what sampling noise alone produces. The ablation below confirms it: adding cross-fitted target encodings of `route_key` and `customer_id` to the logistic regression moves validation AUC from 0.498 to 0.512 and test AUC from 0.488 to 0.489. It is documented, not shipped. `vessel_id` is also usually not known at booking.

## Selection rule

(a) Among the uncalibrated learned candidates (route_history, logreg, hist_gb, random_forest), select the one with the best validation AUC; a tie is broken by the lower validation log loss of the uncalibrated model. Nothing fitted on validation labels takes part in this choice.
Then calibrate the selected base model on validation (sigmoid, base model frozen).
(b) Ship the calibrated model only if, on test, its AUC 95% bootstrap CI lower bound > 0.50 AND its Brier <= prior Brier + 0.003 (i.e. calibration no worse than the base rate within tolerance). Otherwise ship the prior.

The ablation is not eligible: it needs ids the API does not send. (b) looks at test metrics for a ship/no-ship gate, not for choosing between learned models (that is done on validation in (a)). The reason: with 570 validation rows no candidate can clear a strict significance bar, so the gate is "measurably better than chance and honestly calibrated", nothing more. Bootstrap CIs use 1000 resamples.

## Honest limits

- Predictions cluster near the base rate (test scores: min 0.376, p10 0.415, median 0.440, p90 0.463, max 0.507; mean 0.439). Few shipments will ever reach the "high" band.
- Base-rate drift shows up as calibration error: the late rate was 0.399 in train, 0.442 in validation and 0.399 in test, so the shipped mean score 0.439 is off from the test late rate by +0.040 (test ECE 0.043). This is the main thing the monitoring's base-rate and calibration triggers are for.
- F1 alone is misleading here: always predicting "late" scores F1 ≈ 0.57. Lead with ROC-AUC, PR-AUC against the base rate, and Brier.
- The data is synthetic-looking: the booking-time fields barely relate to the outcome. Real signal would need features this dataset does not have (port congestion at departure time, weather, carrier schedule reliability, transhipment count).
- One year of history: `dep_month` sees each month once in train, so any seasonality it learns is a single draw.
- Test covers bookings from 2025-06-01 onwards that have already arrived; late arrivals from the newest bookings are not in it yet, which slightly biases recent windows toward on-time.

## Monitoring

Per prediction, log: request id, timestamp, `model_version`, the raw feature dict, the cleaned features, the score and the risk label. Join to the actual arrival when it lands.

Weekly:
- PSI of the score distribution vs the test-period distribution.
- PSI / share-shift of origin port, destination port and cargo mix.
- Share of categories unseen in training (they score as "infrequent") and of missing / invalid weight and container count.

Once arrivals land (labels lag booking by up to ~50 days): rolling 30-day ROC-AUC with a bootstrap CI, Brier, calibration error (ECE), and the observed late rate.

Retrain triggers:
- rolling AUC CI falls below the `route_history` baseline's AUC on the same window;
- calibration error > 0.05;
- score or input PSI > 0.2;
- observed base rate moves > 5pp from the training base rate;
- otherwise monthly, through a champion/challenger gate: the challenger is trained on the newer time window and must win on a later held-out time slice under the selection rule above.
